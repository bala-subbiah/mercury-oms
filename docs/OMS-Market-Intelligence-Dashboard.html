05 · Into the reimagining sessionKill · Keep · Leapfrog

Observed design patterns to kill, keep, or leapfrog when rebuilding for the AI era.

KILL

  • IE-era / thick-client multi-window navigation and module trees (Ayers GTS web, Avaloq RM Workplace ribbon)
  • Re-keying across OMS / EMS / back office — the "swivel chair"
  • Modal-heavy, form-first order tickets demanding many fields before any validation
  • Training-course-mandatory complexity as a permanent state

KEEP

  • Dense real-time blotter as the trader's home — density is a feature for pros (Bloomberg, CRD)
  • Single golden dataset front-to-back (Enfusion, Limina)
  • Inline pre-trade compliance at the point of trade — an inline alert, never a separate app
  • Exception-based dashboards surfacing only anomalies (Limina)
  • Customizable per-role layouts and profiles (CRD, Enfusion)

LEAPFROG

  • Conversational + command-bar as the primary navigation layer — app-context-aware natural language (Aladdin Copilot model)
  • Natural-language order entry with agentic pre-checks returning a reviewable, compliance-checked draft ticket
  • Notification-driven, mobile-first RM workflows (Engage + Enfusion mobile routing as proof points)
  • Consumer-grade polish at institutional density — moomoo as the aesthetic benchmark
  • AI guardrails as first-class UI: permission-dependent, boundary-limited, trust surfaced in the interface

Caveats. Ratings draw partly on vendor marketing; Domain B ratings for HK-specific features are often Partial/Unknown by necessity, not proof of absence. Ayers cells rely partly on inference from HKEX certification and market share (BCAN tagging rated Partial, not Yes). AI cells count production-shipped features only. Review-site volumes for institutional systems are thin — treat quotes as directional. Figures such as market shares and AUA are vendor self-reported unless attributed to third parties.
